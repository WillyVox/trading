<p>AUSTRAC made a genuinely useful change for everyday crypto users this year: its register of virtual asset service providers (VASPs) — the businesses registered under Australia's anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing rules — became publicly searchable on 30 June 2026.</p>
<h2>What the register actually is</h2>
<p>Previously, checking whether a crypto business was properly registered with AUSTRAC wasn't something the public could easily do directly. The now-public register lists VASPs registered and regulated in Australia, in AUSTRAC's own words improving transparency and helping people identify legitimate businesses before using their services.</p>
<h2>Why this matters right now</h2>
<p>This launch follows a broader 2026 shift in how Australia regulates crypto businesses: since 31 March 2026, the older "digital currency exchange" registration category was replaced by the broader "virtual asset service provider" definition, capturing a wider range of business models. A public register is a natural, useful companion to that expanded scope — it turns a compliance requirement into something an ordinary user can actually act on.</p>
<h2>How to use it before signing up</h2>
<ul>
<li>Search the exact legal or trading name of the platform you're considering</li>
<li>Don't stop at a badge or claim on the provider's own marketing page — confirm it against the register itself</li>
<li>Remember a registration status can change over time, so check again close to when you actually sign up</li>
<li>Treat this as one factor among several — see the next section</li>
</ul>
<h2>What it doesn't tell you</h2>
<blockquote><p>Registration on AUSTRAC's VASP register is an AML/CTF compliance status. It is not a safety rating, not an ASIC financial-product licence, and not a government endorsement of the platform's quality or financial soundness.</p></blockquote>
<p>For what registration does and doesn't actually guarantee, see our fuller explainer on <a href="/crypto/guides/austrac-registration-crypto-exchanges-explained">what AUSTRAC registration means</a>. For the separate question of financial-services licensing, see our piece on <a href="/crypto/guides/asic-digital-asset-licensing-deadline-2026">ASIC's 2026 digital-asset licensing deadline</a>.</p>
<h2>The bigger picture</h2>
<p>A public register is a meaningful step toward making Australia's crypto regulatory environment easier for ordinary users to navigate — but it works best as one input into a broader decision, alongside fees, security practices and how you actually plan to use an exchange. See our full <a href="/crypto/guides/how-to-choose-crypto-exchange-australia">exchange-selection guide</a> for the rest of that picture.</p>

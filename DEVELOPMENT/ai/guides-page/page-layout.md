Yes. I would redesign this page quite substantially **before adding more articles**.

Your current Guides page is technically functional, but visually and strategically it behaves like a sitemap: a heading followed by six links. For a content/SEO/affiliate platform, the Guides homepage should instead become a **content discovery and education hub**.

I reviewed the current approaches used by Canstar, Finder and CoinSpot. Canstar separates hand-picked “Useful Information” from “Latest in cryptocurrency”; Finder builds content around user intent such as beginner platforms, fees, risks and choosing providers; CoinSpot goes further with search, categories, difficulty levels, beginner learning paths and market updates. ([Canstar][1])

Your platform shouldn't copy any one of them. I think we can combine the strongest ideas while making Aus Market more explicitly about **Australian trading education + platform comparison**.

## 1. The biggest problem with the current page

Your current page:

> Guides
> What Is Bitcoin?
> What Does AUSTRAC Registration Actually Mean...?
> Crypto Exchange Fees...
> How to Buy Bitcoin...
> ...

There are several issues.

There is almost **no visual hierarchy** beyond the H1. A highly useful cornerstone article looks identical to a minor article. There is no image, description, publication/update date, category, reading time or indication of whether something is beginner or advanced.

More importantly, the page doesn't tell a new visitor:

> **Where should I start?**

And it doesn't tell an experienced visitor:

> **Where are the things I'm interested in?**

That is the key difference I see between your page and mature financial-content sites.

Canstar, for example, immediately surfaces three important educational actions and then moves into latest content. ([Canstar][1]) CoinSpot explicitly provides “New to crypto? Start here,” recommended beginner learning, market updates, categories and difficulty filters. ([CoinSpot][2])

# 7. Separate Guides from News

This is important for your information architecture.

I wouldn't mix:

**How to Buy Bitcoin in Australia**

with:

**Bitcoin jumps after US inflation data**

Those serve completely different search intent.

I recommend keeping:

### Guides

Evergreen educational content.

### News

Current developments.

### Reviews

Individual exchange/broker reviews.

### Comparisons

Provider-vs-provider or "best X" content.

### Market intelligence

Market information/data.

They can cross-link heavily, but they shouldn't become one giant article feed.

This is also useful commercially.

Consider the funnel:

**Google**

↓

**Educational guide**

“How to Buy Bitcoin in Australia”

↓

**Contextual education**

“What fees should I expect?”

↓

**Relevant comparison**

“Compare Australian crypto exchanges”

↓

**Exchange comparison page**

↓

**Exchange detail/review**

↓

**Affiliate CTA**

“Visit exchange”

That is much closer to the business model you've been building.

## The visual direction I recommend

I actually prefer your existing Aus Market typography and restrained cream/navy/gold palette over trying to make it look exactly like Canstar.

Your branding feels more editorial.

Keep that.

What you're missing is **visual content architecture**, not a completely new visual identity.

I'd use large editorial photography selectively, perhaps 1 prominent hero/featured image plus 6–10 smaller card images. Avoid turning it into a generic crypto site full of glowing Bitcoin coins and neon candlestick charts.

The visual language should communicate:

> **Australian financial research publication**

rather than:

> **Crypto trading website**

That distinction will become increasingly important when you add shares, ETFs, forex, brokers and market intelligence.

---

## One concern I would address now

Don't let `/guides` become `/guides/crypto`.

The six articles in your screenshot are all cryptocurrency-related. That's understandable given the current phase, but the architecture should be designed **now** for the broader Aus Market mission.

I'd make Crypto merely one content pillar:

**Shares · ETFs · Crypto · Forex · Trading Platforms · Investing Basics · Regulation & Safety**

Your database/CMS can have only crypto content today. The UI architecture doesn't have to assume that crypto is the entire business.

That avoids another redesign later.

---

## My preferred MVP

I wouldn't implement the huge page immediately. The first redesign should contain only:

1. **Guides hero + search**
2. **Explore by topic — visual cards**
3. **Start here — beginner guides**
4. **Featured guides**
5. **Latest guides — image cards**
6. **Popular guides**
7. **Compare platforms CTA**
8. **Trust/methodology/disclosure block**

That would already transform the page from what you have now into something much closer to a mature financial-content platform.

Then Phase 2 can introduce learning paths, difficulty filters, sophisticated search, personalization, popular-content analytics and newsletter integration.

Most importantly, I would **not start changing the code yet**. Before implementation, the next useful step is to inspect your existing `Article` model, article repository/query layer, `/guides` implementation, article detail route, categories/topics and CMS fields. Then we can determine which of the design elements above are already supported by your data model versus which require schema changes. That will prevent building a beautiful page whose content has to be hard-coded.

5. ★ How to Check if a Crypto Exchange Is Legit in Australia

I would publish this very early because 2026 gives you a genuinely new angle.

AUSTRAC made its VASP register publicly available on 30 June 2026 specifically so people can verify whether virtual asset service providers are registered.

A short guide could teach:

Before depositing money:

1. Check the exact business name.
2. Search the AUSTRAC VASP register.
3. Check relevant ASIC information where applicable.
4. Verify the website domain.
5. Check fees and withdrawal rules.
6. Enable 2FA.
7. Don't trust social-media links.
8. Be suspicious of guaranteed returns.

This is excellent beginner content because Moneysmart specifically warns about fake exchanges, fake apps, impersonation, social-media approaches and guaranteed returns.

One nuance your article should teach particularly well:

AUSTRAC registration and an AFS licence are not interchangeable concepts.

That's a place where your source-first methodology can genuinely add value.

9. Crypto Tax Australia 2026

This should be short and practical, not pretend to replace an accountant.

The ATO says exchanging crypto for cash, goods or other crypto is likely to constitute a disposal for CGT purposes, and emphasizes keeping transaction records. Its current guidance also discusses staking rewards and airdrops.

I'd structure:

Bought crypto → Sold crypto → Swapped BTC for ETH → Received staking rewards → Moved between your own wallets → What records should I keep?

A simple table would work extremely well.

And prominently:

General information only — individual tax circumstances differ.

Link directly to primary ATO material rather than paraphrasing tax law endlessly.

The first five I would build

I would start only with #1, #2, #3, #4 and #5. Together they create a strong funnel:

How do I start? → Where do I trade? → Can I start small? → What will it cost? → Is this platform legitimate?

Then your comparison/provider database becomes the next logical action rather than an advertisement forced into the article.

I would also keep most guides around 800–1,500 words, use a short key-takeaways block, one clear step sequence, one useful table/diagram, 3–5 contextual internal links, primary Australian sources, a checked/updated date, and a clear next action. The goal shouldn't be “write more words than Finder”; it should be answer the beginner's question faster and with better evidence.

If you approve this direction, the next step I recommend is not writing all ten articles yet.

Yes — the idea is bigger than “make the article prettier.”

What I am proposing is to turn your article system from:

Rich-text blog CMS

into:

Editorial publishing system + verified financial-data system

That distinction is important for AusMarket because fees, supported assets, regulatory status, funding methods and provider features can change. Those facts should ideally come from your structured database, while the editor is responsible for analysis, explanation, examples and narrative.

9. Step 7 — Then introduce database-powered blocks

This is where AusMarket becomes much more interesting.

Suppose an editor writes:

CoinSpot currently lists more than 470 cryptocurrencies.

That number exists in prose.

Three months later CoinSpot has:

510+

Now you have to find:

CoinSpot review
CoinSpot vs Kraken
CoinSpot vs Swyftx
Best exchanges
How to choose an exchange
How to buy crypto

and update them all.

That's the problem.

Instead, allow an editor to insert:

Provider Fact
Provider: CoinSpot
Fact: Supported assets

The stored article could effectively contain:

  "type": "providerFact",
  "providerId": "...",
  "factKey": "SUPPORTED_ASSETS"

Provider
  ↓
ProviderFact
  ↓
latest VERIFIED record
  ↓

and renders:

Supported cryptocurrencies

Verified 14 Sep 2026

Later the provider fact is updated once.

Every article using that dynamic fact becomes current automatically.

11. But do not make everything dynamic

This is important.

```{=html}
<p>
```

Starting with \$100 can make the mechanics of crypto easier to
understand because the numbers are simple. But \$100 is not a
recommendation or a "safe" amount. Crypto remains high risk at any
starting size.

```{=html}
</p>
```

```{=html}
<h2>
```

What is the \$100 example for?

```{=html}
</h2>
```

```{=html}
<p>
```

The useful beginner question is not "How much profit can \$100 make?" It
is "What happens between sending \$100 from my bank and eventually
getting money back out?" That teaches you about fees, order types,
security and records.

```{=html}
</p>
```

```{=html}
<h2>
```

Why fees matter on a small amount

```{=html}
</h2>
```

```{=html}
<p>
```

A percentage fee scales with the transaction, while a fixed cost can
consume a larger share of a small amount. Card funding may also be
priced differently from PayID or bank transfer. A single headline
trading fee therefore does not tell you the full cost.

```{=html}
</p>
```

```{=html}
<h2>
```

Do not choose a coin because its unit price looks cheap

```{=html}
</h2>
```

```{=html}
<p>
```

A coin priced at a few cents is not automatically better value or more
likely to rise than a higher-priced asset. Understand what the asset is
and its risks rather than treating unit price as a shortcut.

```{=html}
</p>
```

```{=html}
<h2>
```

Do not turn the first \$100 into a race

```{=html}
</h2>
```

```{=html}
<p>
```

Social-media hype and urgent messaging can push people to buy before
they understand what they are doing. Slow down, check the provider and
read the confirmation screen.

```{=html}
</p>
```
